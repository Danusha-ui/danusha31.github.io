Calculus in Food Security

Optimizing Paddy Moisture Content via Derivatives $f'(t)$

Context: Malaysian Agriculture

Target Safe Moisture: 14%

🌾 The Bioscience Context: Post-Harvest Preservation

In Malaysia, harvested paddy typically has a high moisture content of around 24%. To ensure long-term Food Security (SFM) and prevent fungal growth or spoilage during silo storage, it must be rapidly and uniformly dried down to a safe level of 14%.

Using standard heated air drying (at roughly 45°C), the moisture loss follows an exponential decay pattern. By applying derivatives, we can find the exact rate of drying (% moisture loss per hour) at any specific time $t$. This prevents over-drying (which cracks the rice kernels, reducing head rice yield) and saves processing energy.

Data Reference Framework: Parameters adapted from realistic Malaysian mechanical paddy drying profiles documented in standard agricultural literature like the "Mardi Research Journal" and MARDI's post-harvest engineering guidelines.

Moisture Function $f(t)$

f(t) = 10 · e-0.15t + 14

Predicts the paddy moisture percentage at hour $t$. The 14 represents the equilibrium/target moisture boundary, while 10 represents the ambient moisture drop required ($24\% - 14\%$).

Drying Rate Derivative $f'(t)$

f'(t) = -1.5 · e-0.15t

Calculated using the Chain Rule. It yields the exact speed of evaporation (% moisture drop per hour) at moment $t$. The negative sign indicates moisture is decreasing.
